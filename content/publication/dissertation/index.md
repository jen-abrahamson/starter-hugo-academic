abstract: Wetlands are critical ecosystems that play an important role in global carbon cycling and methane emission pathways. However, their ephemeral nature—characterized by rapid and dynamic changes in inundation—makes them challenging to monitor and quantify at regional to global scales. This dissertation develops a high-resolution remote sensing fusion framework designed to map dynamic inundation patterns in ephemeral wetlands and estimate associated methane fluxes. By integrating multiple remote sensing data sources and advanced computational methods, this work addresses the challenge of quantifying spatiotemporal dynamics of wetland inundation and their relationship to methane production. The framework provides a scalable approach for monitoring these critical yet dynamic ecosystems, with implications for understanding wetland contributions to atmospheric methane and improving carbon cycle models.
